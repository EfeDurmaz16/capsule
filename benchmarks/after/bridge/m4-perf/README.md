The perf-05 `limit-20` RSS row also repeats in the recheck, 1.007 (1.006 to 1.009, 141.6 MB to 142.6 MB) with `elapsedMs` 0.257, the same cause as the `all` case.

What the two runs agree on, then, are three rows: 10 to 13 ms more wall per process on the two cases whose process ends on a timer (a 2 s delayed sibling, a 3 s idle limit), and about 1 MB more RSS on the Kubernetes collection. The first two were not there in M1's run of the same rows (1.001 and 0.999 in `final-8fdc46c`), so they come with the M3 and M4 commits to `core` (`575c614` one operation context store shared across instances, `2dc1196` attach, `2978dac` hashing after a short read) and `docker`; the candidate's `core` bundle is 18 KB larger and holds 42 KB more heap after import. Importing `core/dist/index.js` and exiting costs the same on both sides (20 alternating pairs at a load of 5.4: 25.3 against 25.6 ms, median ratio 0.998, pair deltas from -4.8 to +4.7 ms), so the 10 to 13 ms is inside the case's own run, between the operations and the process end, and not in module load or exit. It is narrowed to that, not pinned to a line; the two cases' product metrics (`bLatencyMs.p50` 0.071, `settleMs` equal) do not carry it.

Nothing in the rows above is a Capsule hot path that got slower: the three wall-time rows are within 1.5 ms or 10 ms, and every memory row is the harness parent or the correct collection of more data.

The unpaired `run.mjs` spawn (34.3 ms against the record's 29.8 ms) is what a different session gives: in the same session, pair by pair, the M4 worker spawns in the same time as the M3 worker and answers every request in the same time or less, with the same memory, while its bundle is 7 percent larger (`dist` 111223 against 103988 bytes: attach by identity, the per-operation approval binding, the Docker start-timeout rule). The `after/bridge/control` and `review` records put the M3 worker itself within noise of the M1 head on these paths.

Fabric's own bench of the same worker from its side (`fabric-bench-first-attempt-run-test.log` here is the first attempt, which the worker refused at the seed: `fixture sandboxes[0] needs owner_job and operation together, or neither`; Fabric `5e38dfa` seeds the create's operation id and the rerun is Fabric `docs/evidence/perf-ab/m4-bridge`): on the release build, fixture worker spawned and handshake answered 30.3 ms, create round trip 145 us on the in-memory log and 7.0 ms through the journal's two committed writes, exec 148 us; on Docker, spawn 27.3 ms, create 203 ms, exec 84.8 ms, destroy 219 ms; against the Rust double, spawn 1.35 ms, round trip 41 us, journal 7.0 ms. Every in-memory path is 10 to 20 percent under Fabric's M3 record; the journal path is the two fsyncs either way.
